Jooho Kim

PhD Student, School of Statistics, University of Minnesota-Twin Cities

I am a first-year PhD student in the School of Statistics at the University of Minnesota-Twin Cities. I develop statistical methodology for settings where data are incomplete or collected under complex sampling designs, with an emphasis on estimators that are both computationally scalable and statistically efficient.

I received my M.S. in Statistics from Seoul National University, advised by Professor Yei Eun Shin, where my research was supported by a graduate fellowship from the National Research Foundation of Korea. Before that I studied at Korea University, double majoring in Food and Resource Economics and in Statistics.

Research

Research interests

My work so far has been on inference from data that are incomplete by design. In my master's research I showed that two subsamples drawn under different sampling schemes can be analyzed within a single framework once their weights are calibrated to a common target, which recovers efficiency that is otherwise lost when covariates are measured on only part of the cohort.

What interests me about that problem is more general than missing data. I would like to understand how statistical information can be split, borrowed across sources, and recombined without invalidating the inference that follows, and what optimization machinery makes such estimators practical at scale. I am early in my PhD and still exploring which of these directions to pursue.

Broadly: missing and incomplete data · data integration · statistical machine learning · optimization

Papers

Publications and preprints

  1. Kim, J. and Shin, Y. E. (2025). Scalable and efficient multiple imputation for case-cohort studies via influence function-based supersampling. arXiv:2511.14692.

    Revision invited, Biometrics.

    Calibrating the weights of two subsamples drawn under different sampling designs to a common target lets them be analyzed together. This allows an influence function-based supersample to be combined with the original case-cohort subsample, substantially improving the relative efficiency of the resulting estimator.
